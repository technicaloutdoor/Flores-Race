Flores Race

A self-supported traverse of the island of fire, Labuan Bajo to Larantuka
Across Flores on farmers' tracks, over the volcanic spine and back to both coasts: a course concept in ten sections, with its optional loops, its hazards and the villages that will host it.

The concept

The idea in one page

Flores is only about 360 kilometres long, but it is a wall of volcanoes with two coasts. The paved Trans-Flores highway needs roughly twice that distance to cross it because it has to. A race that keeps leaving the highway to climb over the spine on farmers' tracks, drop to a forgotten coast and climb back again reaches well over 1,300 kilometres and more than 30,000 metres of climbing, most of it unpaved and a meaningful share of it on foot with the bike.

The course runs west to east, from Labuan Bajo, the Komodo gateway with international flights and a sunset harbour, to Larantuka, the Portuguese-Catholic town under Ile Mandiri at the island's eastern tip. West to east because the dry-season wind blows from the east-south-east, so riders earn every kilometre, and because the island's story reads that way: from Komodo and the Manggarai highlands to Ngada's megalithic villages, the Lio country around Kelimutu, the ikat weavers of Sikka and the Catholic east.

The format is borrowed deliberately from the Silk Road Mountain Race: one stage, fixed route, no outside support, mandatory checkpoints, a time limit of about two weeks, a dry-season window from late June to September. Some checkpoints are cultural: riders sign in at a village that has agreed to host the race, and the village earns from it. That agreement is the point, not a detail.

Design rules for the course

  1. Prefer the small trackA farm track, a cattle path across savanna or a village-to-village footpath over a ridge always beats a paved road, even a scenic one.
  2. Touch both coasts, repeatedlyEvery section that can cross the spine, does. The island is narrow; the course uses that.
  3. Hike-a-bike is a feature, and it is honestWhere the bike is carried, the section says for how many kilometres, and scouts will have walked it.
  4. Every section has a reasonA volcano, a village, a beach, a piece of history. If a stretch has no story, it is a link, and links are kept short.
  5. Villages are hosts, not sceneryWae Rebo, Bena, Gurusina, Luba, Tololela, Wologai, Nggela and the others receive guests with protocol. No rider passes before the village council has agreed; where agreement is not possible, the route goes around.
  6. Respect the hazard zonesActive volcanoes define exclusion radii the route honours on race day, with a pre-agreed reroute and an abort rule.
  7. Resupply is sparse but never impossibleNo rider should be more than about 120 kilometres from food and water. The north coast and the Nagekeo interior need particular care.

Section 01 · Komodo gate and the Mbeliling forest

The race starts in the one place every rider already half-knows, and turns its back on it within a few kilometres.

Labuan Bajo is dragons, dive boats and an international airport: the busiest harbour in the region, fully geared, expensive in August. The course leaves the waterfront and climbs into the Mbeliling forest reserve, roughly 15,000 hectares of Important Bird Area rising to about 1,275 metres, the highest ground in West Manggarai. It holds all four birds found only on Flores: the Flores hanging parrot, the Flores monarch, the Flores crow and the Flores scops owl. This is the unglamorous, verifiable version of remote, and it is the first test of the course's first rule: the farm track through the forest rather than the highway that skirts it.

Two waterfalls give the section its texture. Cunca Wulang is a river canyon with deep pools and cliff-jumping spots near Wersawe, about 30 kilometres from town, run informally by the village for a small fee: spectacular and easy, no dismount needed. Cunca Rami, a 30-metre fall deep in the forest near Wae Lolos, is a real jungle hike-a-bike whose exact position the desk data itself admits is unresolved, with local sources and the terrain model disagreeing by 400 metres of altitude. That is precisely why this section opens the first scouting season.

The payoff is Sano Nggoang: an elliptical caldera lake at about 750 metres, reported as the largest crater lake in East Nusa Tenggara and among the deepest volcanic lakes anywhere, with solfataras and hot springs steaming on its south-eastern shore below the 903-metre Wai Sano volcano. Most Komodo visitors never see it. The section ends eight kilometres north at Werang, the sub-district capital: a parish church, a boarding school, a police post, a handful of kiosks. The first honest resupply of the race.

Practical

Water
Reliable only in Labuan Bajo. Unreliable at the lake and in Werang; carry from the start.
Resupply
Full in Labuan Bajo (supermarkets, ATMs, the region's only hospital). Kiosks in Werang. Nothing on the forest track.
Sleep
Hotels in Labuan Bajo; small homestays at the lake and in Werang, capacity for a race field unconfirmed; an ecolodge in the Mbeliling landscape.
Protocol
No cultural checkpoint in this section. Village-collected fees at the waterfalls (reported IDR 30,000 to 100,000 at Cunca Wulang depending on guiding).
Hazards
River pools and cliff jumps at Cunca Wulang (informal site, no lifeguard); geothermal features at the lake are not bathing spots; a documented saltwater-crocodile fatality on West Manggarai's coast means no wading in estuaries.

Section 02 · Wae Rebo and the south coast of Manggarai

No road has ever reached Wae Rebo. Every visitor arrives the way the village's own rice and roof thatch do: on foot.

From Werang the course drops off the plateau to a stretch of south coast almost no visitor sees. Dintor is a white-sand beach village in Satar Ruwuk with Pulau Mules offshore and no services; Denge, a few kilometres inland, is the last village a vehicle reaches and the trekkers' staging post, with a rice-field homestay of about nine rooms. Beyond Denge everything moves on foot or on someone's back.

The Wae Rebo trail is about eight kilometres and 700 metres of climbing through rainforest, two and a half to three hours for a trekker, considerably more with a loaded bike. At the top stand seven cone-roofed mbaru niang around a ceremonial ground at about 1,100 to 1,200 metres: five floors of wood, bamboo and palm thatch built without nails, rebuilt by the community in the traditional form rather than in tin, which won the village UNESCO's Asia-Pacific Award for Cultural Heritage Conservation in 2012. Oral tradition names a founder, Empo Maro, who came from Minangkabau in Sumatra guided by a dream. Visitors are received with the Wae Lu'u welcome ceremony, sleep inside the houses and pay a set contribution; roughly IDR 350,000 for an overnight package and a IDR 50,000 ceremony donation are the figures currently reported, and there is no visitor quota. None of that can simply be inherited by a race: the village council's agreement is the precondition for this checkpoint.

The onward trail from Wae Rebo towards Todo is the section's open question; no public trekking source describes it, and every visitor account goes back down to Denge. The course therefore carries an out-and-back alternative. Todo, reached by road, is where Manggarai tradition places the seat of its old kingdom and its oldest standing mbaru niang, the drum house Niang Todo: the same architecture as Wae Rebo, with the origin story attached, and elders who receive guests with a guest book and a small donation. The section ends at Iteng, the Satar Mese sub-district capital on the coast: a market, a parish, a health centre, the first real food after two remote days.

Practical

Water
Unreliable throughout. Denge and Wae Rebo have water for guests, not for a field of riders; carry full capacity from Werang.
Resupply
Minimal: kiosks in Denge and Dintor, nothing on the trail, a market at Iteng.
Sleep
Homestays in Denge and inside the mbaru niang at Wae Rebo (by agreement); nothing at Dintor or Todo; Iteng unconfirmed.
Protocol
Cultural checkpoint. Welcome ceremony, guest book, set contributions; photography of interiors and ceremonies by permission. A race-specific agreement with the council is required before a single rider passes.
Hazards
Steep, slippery porters' path with steps; landslides after rain; heat on the coast, cold at the village at night.

Section 03 · Manggarai highlands

Roughly 1,100 metres of climbing separate the coast at Iteng from Ruteng's cool air, and the section only gets higher from there.

Ruteng sits at about 1,200 metres: a compact, walkable Catholic hill town of red roofs and rice terraces, sixteen to twenty-four degrees, the best full resupply of the whole west (a central market, a hospital, banks, many homestays). Its cathedral, consecrated in 2002, seats 2,500; the Golo Curu hill above the town carries a Marian grotto and the panorama. On the most Catholic island of Indonesia's most Catholic province, this is where the contrast with the customary-law villages just behind the riders is sharpest.

A short detour west of town, the Cancar rice fields resolve from a viewing hill into a spider's web. This is lingko, the Manggarai communal land system: a field divided outward from a centre point, the lodok, into wedges whose width reflects each family's standing, with planting and harvest rites at the centre. It is the one spectacular view on the whole course that costs no hike-a-bike at all, for a fee of a few thousand rupiah and a ten-minute walk.

South-east of Ruteng rises the roof of Flores: Poco Mandasawu, the island's highest point at about 2,370 metres, and Poco Ranaka with its 1987 lava dome Anak Ranakah, inside the 32,000-hectare Ruteng Nature Recreation Park. The course skirts this massif on the way to Ranamese, a small, 21-metre-deep crater lake at 1,220 metres with a warung at the car park: the highest and coldest rest stop of the race, with the Cunca Rede waterfall a short trail away. Then it descends north to Liang Bua, a single wide limestone chamber where, in September 2003, a joint Indonesian-Australian team found the near-complete skeleton of a one-metre-tall human relative, Homo floresiensis. A 2016 re-dating put the youngest bones at 60,000 to 100,000 years, and at Mata Menge in the So'a basin the lineage reaches back about 700,000 years. The cave is still used by the local community for ceremonies; a guide meets visitors at the entrance, and a small museum shows casts.

Practical

Water
Reliable in Ruteng; unreliable at Iteng, Ranamese and Liang Bua. Nothing on the massif.
Resupply
Full in Ruteng. A warung at Ranamese. Minimal at Liang Bua.

Hazards
Sustained climb from sea level to 1,200 metres in heat, then cold and cloud at altitude; exposed volcanic trail if the massif is crossed on foot.

Section 04 · The forgotten north coast

Dry, hot, sparsely populated and beautiful: the section the concept names as its defining unknown.

Reo is an old north-coast market town with a mosque and a church side by side, a bank, a health centre and a Dutch-era cannon on the hill above the bay; its working harbour, Kedindi, a few kilometres west at the river mouth, is talked of as the province's next big export port. Local history traces the first settlement to the Bima sultanate's traders; under the Dutch, the Manggarai resistance leader Motang Rua was shipped through Reo into exile. It is the last full resupply before the driest stretch of the course.

East of Reo the coast becomes lontar-palm savanna and wide bays with almost nobody in them. The desk data called this corridor undocumented; current sources say a road exists the whole way, paved from Ruteng to Reo and Pota, then rough, potholed and periodically cut by landslides between Pota and Riung via Buntal, Golo Lijung and Dampek. The honest open question is not whether a track exists but how good it is with a loaded bike, and above all where the water is. In August 2026 the district was under an emergency dry-season water-trucking operation covering Pota, Nangambaur, Golo Lijun and posts across Sambi Rampas and Borong. Water here is not a hypothesis; it is the constraint.

Pota, the Sambi Rampas sub-district capital, is the midpoint and the junction where the Manggarai Timur interior loop rejoins the coast. Riung is the reward: a small fishing port at the gate of the Seventeen Islands Marine Park, more than twenty coral islets reached by hired boat, with a colony of thousands of flying foxes that lifts off Pulau Ontoloe at dusk, snorkelling over reef, and half a dozen simple guesthouses. Park entrance is reported at about IDR 100,000; a day boat for a small group around IDR 400,000 per person.

Water
The constraint of the race. Reliable in Reo only; unreliable at Pota and Riung; villages between were on emergency trucking in August 2026. Mandatory carry capacity; planned water points only if unavoidable.
Resupply
Basic in Reo and Pota (kiosks, health posts), basic in Riung with guesthouse kitchens. Nothing in between.

No cultural checkpoint. Marine park fee if riders take a boat. Mosque and church etiquette in Reo.
Hazards
Heat and dehydration; rough, landslide-prone road east of Pota; river mouths and mangroves are crocodile country, no wading at dawn or dusk.

Section 05 · Ngada: megaliths under Inerie

The villages beneath Inerie orient their ancestor shrines towards its slope. The mountain is a presiding relative, not scenery.

From the coast at Riung the course climbs to the Bajawa plateau, eleven hundred metres up, with a stop halfway that riders will remember: the Mengeruda hot springs at Soa, warm river pools in a jungle valley near the airstrip, ticketed at a few thousand rupiah and open eight to six. Bajawa itself is a cool highland capital with a market, hotels and ATMs, the last full service before four days of minimal-service villages. Wolobobo hill above the town is the plateau's best sunrise: mist pooling below, Inerie's silhouette, Ebulobo far to the east on a clear morning.

Nineteen kilometres south, Bena is the reference image of Ngada culture: two rows of thatched houses on a narrow ridge framing a stone-terraced square of megalithic tombs and paired ngadhu and bhaga, a carved post for each clan's male ancestor and a small thatched house for the female one, nine pairs for nine clans. Guests sign a book and leave a donation (IDR 10,000 to 25,000 is commonly cited). Luba, a few hundred metres away, has four clans and carved animal motifs and far fewer visitors; Tololela, on a steep hillside at 650 metres, is where travellers actually sleep in the village; Gurusina, on Inerie's south-eastern flank, was rebuilt in traditional materials after a fire on 13 August 2018 destroyed 27 of its 33 houses while the village watched a football match. Each has its own council, and each checkpoint needs its own agreement. Reba, the Ngada new-year festival of yams and ancestors, runs from late December into January: spectacular, and outside the race window.

Inerie is 2,245 metres, the highest peak in Ngada, dormant since a smoke plume in 1911, and climbed from Watumeze in three to four hours over loose scree with no shade and more than 1,500 metres of gain. The main course crosses its shoulder from Bena down to the coast at Aimere, five to ten kilometres of steep, loose hike-a-bike that nobody has yet done with a loaded bike. The Inerie full circuit is the alternative: Bena, Gurusina, the wild southern slope down to the sea at Sewowoto, then the coast road west. Aimere closes the section as a working port: the ferry to Sumba and Kupang, and household stills turning lontar palm sap into arak over open fires.

Reliable in Bajawa and Soa; unreliable in every village under the volcano and at Aimere.

Hazards

Section 06 · Ebulobo and the Nagekeo plains

Back up to the plateau, round the cone of Ebulobo, and down the Aesesa valley to the north coast.

Mataloko is the section's postcard: a whitewashed colonial-era minor seminary, St Yohanes Berchmans, silhouetted against the steam of a geothermal field of more than twenty hot springs, fumaroles and mud pools across a thousand hectares. The springs are a real rider amenity; the geothermal plant behind them, operating since 1998, is also a live local-goodwill issue in the surrounding villages, which is worth knowing before any checkpoint or filming near it. Wogo, a sealed side road away, has nine ngadhu-bhaga pairs in a U-shaped compound and, a kilometre's walk further, the abandoned original site of old Wogo where megalithic tombs still stand in the grass and still serve ceremonies.

Boawae sits directly on the north-western slope of Ebulobo, an active 2,124-metre stratovolcano first recorded erupting in 1830, monitored from an observation post at Ekowolo, farmed almost to the treeline. A summit hike from Mulakoli takes about two hours of forest and a final scramble, with a village-mandated guide. Boawae is also the home of Etu, Nagekeo's ceremonial one-handed boxing, fought in palm-fibre gloves as part of the harvest-thanksgiving cycle in June and July: a living ritual, not a show, and one whose dates may fall inside the race window.

The Aesesa valley then opens into the largest rice plain of Flores, irrigated, flat and hot, and Mbay, the Nagekeo capital, with hotels and the last certain full resupply before the waist crossing. Its port, Marapokot, eight kilometres north, runs ferries to Palue and towards Maumere. Two alternatives shorten this section: straight from Boawae over to the south coast at Nangaroro, or the whole way along the remote south coast from Aimere to Nangaroro, skipping the plateau altogether.

Section 07 · The blue-stone coast to Ende

The narrowest part of Flores is short on the map and hard on the ground.

The crossing from the north-coast plain at Mbay to Nangaroro on the south coast is the least verified link of the whole course: a computed candidate over the island's waist, mostly unpaved, with about 1,700 metres of climbing packed into fifty-odd kilometres, and no source that confirms the specific line. It is scouting-priority terrain, not a confirmed road. Nangaroro itself is a farming and fishing district of grey-stone beaches; a homestay, unreliable water, and the last bed before Ende.

Twenty-five kilometres before Ende comes the section's headline: Penggajawa, a beach made not of sand but of smooth blue-green volcanic pebbles, called Batu Biru or Batu Hijau depending on whether they are wet. Local accounts differ on whether the stones come down the rivers from the mountains or up from the seafloor; what is certain is that residents hand-sort them to size for a small export trade, without machinery. Nobody sleeps here; it is a stop and a photograph.

Ende is the largest town of central Flores, about 90,000 people under the 637-metre Iya volcano, with a hospital, an airport, hotels, the Mbongawani market where traders wear woven sarongs, and the strongest history hook of the middle course. Sukarno lived here in Dutch-imposed exile from January 1934 to 1938, in a plain 1927 house on Jalan Perwira that has been a museum since 1954, its meditation room and library largely unchanged, free entry by donation. On the seafront a five-branched breadfruit tree, planted in 1980 to replace the original, marks where he is said to have worked out the five principles of the Pancasila while looking at the sea. Full resupply, and the gateway to the signature section.

Section 08 · Kelimutu and the Lio country

The only place on the course where riders are required to stop. In Lio belief it is where souls go.

Out of Ende the road climbs past Wolotopo, a Lio village stepped up a steep hillside above the sea where households still weave and keep their ceremonial houses, into cool highland farmland. The Detusoko terraces at 800 metres are compared with Ubud's and see a fraction of the visitors; Wologai, higher still at about 1,045 metres, is an 800-year-old circular village around a stepped stone ceremonial ground, rebuilt in traditional form after a 2012 fire, where elders receive guests with betel and a guest book and the annual Nggua Ria thanksgiving is still held. Moni, one road of homestays and warungs at the foot of the mountain, is where riders sleep before the dawn climb.

Kelimutu's three crater lakes sit at 1,639 metres on one summit inside a 5,300-hectare national park: Tiwu Ata Mbupu, the lake of the old people; the middle lake of young men and maidens; Tiwu Ata Polo, the bewitched lake. Each shifts colour independently over months as the hydrothermal chemistry changes; in July 2026 all three were shades of green. The Lio hold that the lakes sort the dead by how they lived, and every 14 August the mosalaki process to the rim for Pati Ka, feeding the ancestral spirits with red rice, pork, betel, tobacco and palm wine before a circle dance. The park opens at five in the morning; the foreign entrance fee is reported at IDR 150,000 on weekdays and 225,000 at weekends. Standing rules apply regardless of alert level: no approach to the rims, above all at the middle crater, no camping, dawn only. PVMBG recorded a rise in the middle lake's temperature through June 2026 and a January 2026 wall slide, and kept the volcano at Level I; the status is re-checked the week before the race, never assumed.

South of the mountain the course drops through the Lio weaving villages. Jopu and Wolojita share the back-strap loom tradition but now mostly dye with chemicals; Nggela, on a coastal ridge at 180 metres reached by a single-track road, is the village that defines Lio ikat, almost every household at a loom, its cloth collected internationally, and even here truly natural morinda-root dyeing, a two-year process, is rare and demonstrated rather than routine. Nggela is a named cultural checkpoint in the concept and needs council agreement like Wae Rebo. The section ends on white sand: Koka's twin coves below Wolowiro, two kilometres of unpaved spur through cocoa forest with a small private toll, and Paga's simple beachside warungs on the highway.

Section 09 · Sikka and the Portuguese south

The quiet older half of the Portuguese story that the finish town will complete.

Sikka village gave its name to the regency and to a royal line, the Ximenes da Silva, whose descendants still live here: a kingdom whose rulers converted under Portuguese and Dominican influence in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries and kept real authority into the twentieth, long after Portugal ceded the coast to the Dutch in 1859. Its wooden church of St Ignatius Loyola, begun in 1896 and first used for Mass on Christmas Eve 1899, blends a European Baroque form with ikat-motif interior decoration and teak shipped from Java. Every Good Friday the village carries a crucifix said to be a royal heirloom through the streets in the Logu Senhor procession. Sikka weavers make some of the island's most elaborate ikat, dark palettes with sailors, boats, shrimp and crabs from a seafaring ancestry.

Lela, the next sub-district capital along the coast, is a secondary seat of the same mission and a natural link into Maumere; a nineteen-metre Mary of All Nations statue stands on a hill in the area, its exact position relative to the route still to be confirmed. Nine kilometres inland from Maumere, on a hilltop, is Ledalero: St Paul's Major Seminary, founded in 1937 by the Divine Word Missionaries, one of the largest Catholic seminaries in the world, which has formed close to 1,500 missionary priests.

Maumere is the largest town of eastern Flores and the section's finish: airport, the TC Hillers hospital, market, hotels, a natural rest day before the hardest logistics of the race. At half past five in the morning on 12 December 1992 a magnitude 7.7 earthquake off the north coast sent tsunami waves ashore within minutes, killing about 2,000 people, some 700 of them on Babi island where the run-up reached 26 metres. The town's young building stock is that legacy. On the way in, Wuring is a Bajo, or mixed Bajo and Bugis, village built on stilts over the tidal flats, plank walkways instead of streets, boats under the houses: a working community to ride through slowly and respectfully, not a checkpoint.

Section 10 · Egon and the far east

Spectacular and dangerous are the same fact here. The route is designed around it.

Eighteen kilometres south-east of Maumere, Watublapi is the most arrangeable cultural stop of the whole course: not an adat village to be petitioned but a weavers' cooperative, Sanggar Bliran Sina, founded in the 1980s with the help of a German priest, now more than forty members who spin local cotton, dye it with indigo and morinda from their gardens, tie the resist patterns and weave on back-strap looms, and welcome groups with dance and music by arrangement. Buying cloth is how a visiting group says thank you.

Egon is the far east's hike-a-bike: an active volcano of about 1,661 to 1,703 metres, sources disagree, with an acid crater lake and fumaroles, climbed from an unsigned trailhead beyond Blidit at about 790 metres, roughly four hours up through forest onto bare slopes and four down. It reawakened in 2004, forcing 7,000 people off its flanks, and has been quiet at Level I since; a river hot spring five kilometres along the access road is the reward for those who do not summit. Then the black-sand coast at Waiterang and Wodong, small dive resorts and Pulau Babi offshore, and Talibura, a mangrove-fringed village that is the last basic resupply before the longest push of the race.

Lewotobi Laki-laki, 1,584 metres, and Lewotobi Perempuan, 1,703 metres, two kilometres apart, have been in violent eruption since 3 November 2024, when the first eruption of the crisis killed nine or ten people and displaced more than 10,000. The alert reached the maximum Level IV more than once, most recently in September 2025; in 2026 alone MAGMA Indonesia logged 778 eruptions by late August, the second most active volcano in the country. Ash has repeatedly closed Maumere's airport and blanketed the Trans-Flores through Talibura. As of the last week of August 2026 PVMBG held Level III with a five-kilometre exclusion radius and standing lahar warnings for named rivers. The course therefore stays on the north coast, well outside the radius, and treats the highway corridor via Boru as an alternative to be opened only by a race-week go/no-go decision against the live bulletin, with an abort rule agreed in advance. Larantuka is the finish: a Portuguese-Catholic port converted in the 1500s, where the Confreria has run the Semana Santa processions for more than four hundred years, carrying the Tuan Ma statue, found on the beach around 1510, from its chapel to the Reinha Rosari cathedral each Holy Week, and where some residents still speak a Portuguese-influenced Malay creole. Above it stands Ile Mandiri, 1,502 metres, the ancestral mountain of the old kingdom, four to five hours from Riang Kemie: the optional sting in the tail, if it can be shown safe after two weeks of racing.

Earthquakes and tsunamiThe 12 December 1992 M7.7 earthquake and tsunami killed about 2,000 people around Maumere and on Babi island, with a 26-metre run-up at Riang-Kroko. On 15 August 2026 a M7.7 earthquake 68 kilometres north-north-west of Ende killed at least 47 people, damaged hundreds of houses in Nagekeo and produced more than 230 aftershocks; a tsunami warning was lifted the same day. Check road and bridge damage reports in Nagekeo before planning near Boawae; carry a tsunami awareness note for the whole north coast.
Water and heatThe north coast and the Nagekeo interior were on emergency water trucking in August 2026. Mandatory carry capacity, coasts ridden early and late, a heat-illness plan, planned water points only where unavoidable.
Malaria and dengueEast Nusa Tenggara is among Indonesia's highest-malaria provinces; Sikka regency was named among the concentrations in 2023, while several Flores districts hold elimination certificates. Prophylaxis advice from a travel clinic, repellent, nets at checkpoints, and symptom awareness in the briefing.
SeasonLate June to September is the dry, cooler south-east monsoon; August is the driest month. Coastal nights around 19 to 20 °C, highland towns noticeably colder; Ruteng averages about 16 °C. Landslides carry from the wet season into early dry season on cut slopes.
AnimalsFree-roaming village dogs everywhere on course (standard bikepacking advice, rabies pre-exposure vaccination to be considered). Saltwater crocodiles are documented on Flores' coasts, including a fatality in West Manggarai: no wading or swimming at river mouths, mangroves or estuaries, especially at dawn and dusk. Komodo dragons are on the islands, not on the course.
RoadsThe Trans-Flores is used only for short links and never at night in towns; ash has reduced visibility on it through Talibura, Ile Bura and Titehena during eruptive phases. Off the highway, road quality is often poor and better suited to motorbikes than cars, which is the point.
Story and protocol

Cultural threads and protocol

The course is built to carry a handful of stories from one end of the island to the other. They are also the reason the race must arrive as a guest.

Deep time
Liang Bua, Mata Menge

A one-metre-tall human relative excavated in 2003, dated to 60,000 to 100,000 years; and, in the So'a basin, a jaw and teeth from about 700,000 years ago. Flores was an island of its own evolution long before anyone now living.

The megalithic present
Bena, Luba, Tololela, Gurusina, Wogo; Wae Rebo, Todo; Cancar

Ngadhu and bhaga shrines, one pair per clan; conical mbaru niang houses; spider-web fields divided from a centre. Living social orders made visible in stone, thatch and rice.

Weaving
Nggela, Jopu, Wolojita; Watublapi; Manggarai songke

Three living ikat traditions: Lio, Sikka and Manggarai. Back-strap looms, resist-tied thread, natural dyes where they survive (Watublapi still uses them; the coastal Lio villages mostly do not). Buying cloth is the honest way to support a village.

Fire and souls
Kelimutu; every volcano on the route

The three lakes as the sorting place of the dead in Lio belief, fed every 14 August at Pati Ka. Ile Mandiri as the ancestor of the Larantuka kingdom. Inerie faced by every shrine beneath it.

Portugal and Rome
Sikka, Lela, Ledalero, Larantuka, Solor

A Dominican stone fort on Solor in 1566; a wooden church at Sikka in 1899; a seminary at Ledalero that has formed 1,500 priests; five centuries of Semana Santa at Larantuka. More than four in five people on Flores are Catholic.

The republic
Ende

Sukarno's exile years, 1934 to 1938, and the breadfruit tree where the Pancasila is said to have been shaped.

Combat as ritual
Cancar and Manggarai (caci); Boawae (Etu)

Whip-and-shield fighting at the Penti harvest festival; one-handed boxing in palm-fibre gloves in the Nagekeo harvest cycle in June and July. Attended only by consent, never staged.

Sea people
Wuring; the Bajo coast

A stilt village of Bajo and Bugis families over the tidal flats of Maumere bay, one of very few on an island of highland farmers.

Arriving as a guest

  1. Arrive slowly and greet the elders or the head of the household before anything else; a checkpoint in a village exists because the council said yes.
  2. Sign the guest book and leave the donation or contribution the village has set; where betel (sirih) is offered, accept it.
  3. Ask before photographing people, house interiors, ceremonies or the shrines; some structures are restricted.
  4. Dress modestly in villages and churches; no swimwear away from the beach.
  5. No naked flames, stoves or smoking near thatched houses. Gurusina lost 27 houses in one evening in 2018; Wologai lost 22 in 2012.
  6. Buy cloth, food and beds from the village itself; that income is the reason the race is welcome.
  7. Working communities that have not agreed to host (Wuring, the seminaries, the geothermal villages) are passed through, quietly, not treated as checkpoints.

Data and method

Course geometry: data/segments.geojson and data/routes.json in the project repository, routed over the Overture Maps road network (OpenStreetMap-derived) with the remote cost profile; anchors from data/nodes.geojson; places from data/pois.geojson with their own confidence and sources.

Elevation: SRTM 1 arc-second tiles (USGS), sampled every 50 m, median-and-mean smoothed, 10 m hysteresis threshold for climbing; bathymetry clamped to sea level.

Maps: rendered by pipeline/render_brochure_maps.py; relief from SRTM, coastline, lakes and roads © OpenStreetMap contributors via Overture Maps.

Imagery: contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2025–2026, processed by ESA; cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs from the AWS Open Data registry (Element 84).

Desk research: a search pass in September 2026 over travel, news, official (PVMBG, MAGMA Indonesia, BNPB, USGS, GVP) and academic sources, recorded with confidence labels in the project's working files; volcano and earthquake facts are dated in the text.

Caveats

Every segment is concept status. Distances and climbing will change with scouted GPS tracks; the climbing threshold is a design choice still to be calibrated.

Track and path share on most computed pairs is low because OpenStreetMap knows Flores' roads far better than its footpaths. The remoteness the course is after may exist where the map is empty; only feet on the ground will tell.
